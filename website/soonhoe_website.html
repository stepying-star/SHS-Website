FAQ

Common questions

General information as of 2026, not legal or tax advice. Rules change — we confirm the latest position for your case.

How long does it take to set up a Singapore company?

Once documents and identity checks are ready, ACRA usually approves an online application within a day; most clients are incorporated within 1–3 working days. A private limited company needs at least one shareholder, one director ordinarily resident in Singapore, a minimum paid-up capital of S$1, a local registered address, and a company secretary appointed within six months.

Can a foreigner own 100% of a Singapore company?

Yes. Foreign individuals or companies can hold all the shares. The company still needs at least one director who is ordinarily resident in Singapore — if you don't have one yet, a nominee director arrangement can be considered.

What are the yearly compliance requirements?

For most private companies: hold the AGM within 6 months of the financial year end (unless exempt or dispensed with); file the Annual Return with ACRA within 7 months of year end; file Estimated Chargeable Income (ECI) with IRAS within 3 months of year end unless exempt; and file Form C-S / Form C by 30 November. GST returns apply if you are GST-registered, and financial statements may need to be filed in XBRL.

Does my company need an audit?

Not always. A company that qualifies as a "small company" — generally meeting at least two of: annual revenue up to S$10 million, total assets up to S$10 million, and up to 50 employees — can be exempt from statutory audit. Where an audit is required, it is carried out with our licensed audit partners.

What is the difference between an Employment Pass (EP) and the Global Investor Programme (GIP)?

An EP is a work pass from the Ministry of Manpower: a Singapore employer applies for you, and you must meet the qualifying salary (from S$5,600 a month in 2026, higher with age and in financial services, rising to S$6,000 from 1 January 2027) and pass the COMPASS points framework. The GIP, run by the Economic Development Board, grants permanent residence to established business owners and investors who meet its profile criteria and invest through one of three options — at least S$10 million in a Singapore business, S$25 million in a GIP-select fund, or a single family office with S$200 million in assets. GIP approval is discretionary.

Which route is right for me?

If you plan to run or work in a Singapore company, an EP (or EntrePass) is usually the practical starting point, and you can apply for PR later. The GIP suits founders of substantial businesses who can commit a large investment. We review your profile first and tell you honestly which routes are realistic.

Do you handle everything in-house?

Accounting, tax, corporate secretarial and HR work is handled by our own team. Regulated work — such as statutory audit, legal advice, licensed property agency or fund matters — is carried out by licensed partners, with us coordinating so you still have one point of contact.

How do you protect my information?

We handle personal data in line with Singapore's Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA), share it only as needed to deliver your service, and keep every engagement confidential. See our Privacy Policy for details.
